## Law 25 in brief

> This article provides general information. It is not legal advice. For your situation, consult a lawyer.

The Act to modernize legislative provisions as regards the protection of personal information, commonly called “Law 25,” was passed by Québec’s National Assembly in September 2021. Among other things, it amends the Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector, which governs businesses. As set out in the Act itself, its obligations came into force in stages, in September 2022, 2023 and 2024. The Commission d’accès à l’information (CAI) is the body responsible for overseeing its application.

Personal information is any information that concerns a natural person and allows them to be identified, directly or indirectly: a name in a spreadsheet, an email address in a follow-up list, a note in a client record. And every automation moves this kind of information from one tool to another.

## The obligations that affect your automations

### A designated person in charge

By default, the person with the highest authority in the business is the person in charge of the protection of personal information. They may delegate this function in writing, in whole or in part. The title and contact information of the person in charge must be published on the business’s website.

### Valid consent

Consent must be manifest, free, enlightened and given for specific purposes. In practice: a box left unchecked by default, a purpose stated in plain terms and a separate request for each purpose. If your contact form is also used to feed your newsletter, that is a distinct purpose: present it separately.

### Technologies turned off by default

Technologies that make it possible to identify, locate or profile a person must be turned off by default, and the person must be informed of them. Check the settings of your marketing tools before turning on their tracking.

### A privacy impact assessment

A privacy impact assessment (PIA) is required, among other cases, before communicating personal information outside Québec, and for any project to acquire, develop or overhaul an information system or electronic service delivery system involving personal information. An automation project often falls into this second category. And many online applications host their data outside Québec.

### An incident register

A business must keep a register of its confidentiality incidents: unauthorized access, loss, communication to the wrong person. If an incident presents a risk of serious injury, it must notify the CAI and the persons concerned. A misconfigured flow, for example an email sent to the wrong list, can be an incident.

### Data portability

Since September 2024, the last stage set out in the Act, a person can ask that computerized personal information collected from them be communicated to them in a structured, commonly used technological format, or transferred to a third party authorized to collect it. To respond, you need to know which tools hold that information.

### Automated decisions

When a decision about a person is based exclusively on automated processing, for example a request accepted or refused with no human involvement, the business must inform them. At their request, it must also tell them what information was used and the main factors that led to the decision, and allow them to submit observations to a person able to review it.

## Six habits for automating in keeping with Law 25

- **Map your data flows.** Before connecting two applications, draw the path: what information leaves from where, goes where, when, and who has access at each step. This map serves the project, the PIA and any access or portability requests.
- **Minimize data.** Only move what the flow needs. An invoice reminder doesn’t need the client’s date of birth. Less information in motion means less exposure if an incident occurs.
- **Give named accounts, with least privilege.** Every person and every integration has its own account, with only the rights it needs. No shared passwords and no generic administrator accounts. A service provider’s access is removed at the end of the engagement.
- **Document.** Record what each flow does, what information it touches, where it’s stored and how long it’s kept. This documentation helps you demonstrate your practices and keeps any single person from holding all the knowledge.
- **Know where your data is hosted.** For each tool, check where the data is hosted and processed. If it’s outside Québec, the PIA comes before the communication, and the communication must be covered by a written agreement.
- **Set a framework for AI.** An assistant that sorts requests or summarizes files processes personal information. Keep a human in the loop for any decision that affects a person, check what the provider does with the data sent (retention, reuse) and document the use.

## Where to start

1. Confirm who your person in charge is and publish their title and contact information.
2. Take inventory of your current data flows, including spreadsheets and attachments.
3. Identify the tools that host data outside Québec and plan the required PIAs.
4. Open your incident register, even if it’s empty.
5. Review your forms: boxes unchecked by default, clear purposes, a link to your privacy policy.

## In conclusion

Law 25 doesn’t hold automation back. It asks you to know what moves, where and why. Done well, an automation project is even an opportunity to put your information in order: fewer copies, fewer open access points, more documentation.
